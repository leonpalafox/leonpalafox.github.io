Where 15-year-olds gained ground, and where they lost it

PISA 2025 tested about 760 000 students across 91 education systems. Each nationally covered system is shaded by how far its mean score moved — blue for a rise, red for a fall — against the previous cycle, or across the past decade. These are changes in system averages, not the progress of the same students. Score levels appear in the table below.

How to read this

2022 → 2025 is the difference between the two most recent cycles. Decade trend is the OECD’s average decennial trend: a linear trend fitted across the PISA cycles from 2015 to 2025 and expressed as score points per decade, so it is not simply the 2015–2025 gap.

The OECD flags a change as statistically significant when it is distinguishable from zero at the 95% level. Systems whose change is not significant are hatched on the map and marked “NS” in the table — a non-significant estimate does not establish that performance was unchanged.

Regional samples use locator circles instead of shading their parent country: Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu and Zhejiang; 17 of Ukraine’s 27 regions; Dushanbe; and Iraqi Kurdistan. Select or focus a marker for coverage notes. Cyprus covers the area under the effective control of the Republic of Cyprus.

An asterisk marks a sampling caution. Albania’s trends are excluded under OECD reporting guidance. Viet Nam’s trends are unavailable after a change in test instruments. Uzbekistan’s reading and mathematics are withheld. Guatemala and Paraguay’s test-mode changes add uncertainty beyond the reported standard errors. See the Reader’s Guide, pp. 16–20.
